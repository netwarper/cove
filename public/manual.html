Cove — User Manual

A private, encrypted place for your meeting notes and tasks. Everything runs locally and stays on your own storage.

Manual

1. What this is

A self-contained notes-and-tasks app with nothing to sign up for.

Cove keeps a running thread of daily notes per workspace, a unified Tasks system (due dates, priorities, recurrence), rich-text notes with images and attachments, optional meeting recording & transcription, and full-text search — all encrypted at rest and served by a tiny local server with zero third-party runtime dependencies.

  • Local & private. Your notes never leave your machine unless you choose to sync the data folder or send yourself something over Slack.
  • Encrypted. Everything is stored with AES-256-GCM; the key is derived from your passphrase and only held in memory while you're unlocked.
  • Portable. The whole folder can be copied to any machine with Node.js and started immediately.
Tip: Point the data directory at a cloud-sync folder (Google Drive, Dropbox, Box) and the same encrypted vault follows you across devices.

2. Getting started

First launch — set your passphrase

The first time you open the app it asks you to create a passphrase. This is the key to everything — it derives the encryption key for your vault. Choose something strong (at least 8 characters; a memorable phrase is better than a short password).

There is no password reset. If you forget your passphrase and lose your recovery key, your notes cannot be decrypted by anyone — that's the point of the encryption. Save the recovery key.

Save your recovery key

Right after setup you're shown a one-time recovery key. It's a second way into your vault if you forget your passphrase. Write it down or store it in a password manager. You can't see it again later.

Unlocking

On later visits you unlock with your passphrase (or recovery key, or biometrics if you enrolled — see Security). After a period of inactivity the app auto-locks and you'll unlock again.

4. Workspaces

Workspaces are independent streams of notes — one per project, team, or area of life. Each keeps its own notes, its own tasks, and its own default template.

  • Switch with the dropdown at the top of the sidebar.
  • Create / rename / delete via the ⚙ button next to the workspace dropdown.
  • A brand-new workspace shows a landing page with tips instead of auto-creating an empty note.
General is the default workspace and is always present.

5. Notes

Daily vs. scratch

  • New Daily starts a dated note that carries your Ongoing Notes forward from the previous daily note — your running thread.
  • New scratch note is a clean Meeting-Notes page for a quick jot. It never affects the ongoing thread.
  • Re-date a note: click the date at the top of a note to pick a new one — handy to label a note you forgot to make yesterday, or to prep one for tomorrow. This changes only the displayed date; a note keeps its place in the carry-forward chain (which follows the order notes were created), so your running thread never silently rewires.
  • One daily per day: if you hit New Daily when you already have a daily note dated today (or a day you pre-dated ahead), Cove offers to open that one instead of starting a duplicate — you can still choose Create another.
  • Today's-daily reminder: if you've been working in an older note for a while and haven't started a daily note for today, Cove shows a quiet, dismissible banner suggesting a ＋ New Daily. It appears at most once a day per device — dismiss it or ignore it and it won't nag again. Tune how long "a while" is (default 10 seconds, up to 10 minutes) — or turn it off — under Settings → Daily-note reminder.
  • Resize the task lists: when your Overdue & Today or Upcoming list gets long it scrolls at a set height instead of pushing the page down. Drag the grip below either list to make both windows taller or shorter (double-click to reset); the size is shared by both lists and remembered per device.

6. Tasks

One unified, Todoist-style system for to-dos and reminders.

Tasks live at the workspace level — not on a single note — so the same task shows on whichever daily note you're looking at. Each task has a due date, a priority (P1–P4), and optional recurrence.

Adding a task (quick-add)

Type into the box under ✔ Overdue & Today. You can write naturally — the due date, priority, and repeat are parsed locally, with no AI — or set them with the pickers. The Repeat picker also lets you set an interval (every N days / weeks / months) and, for weekly, tap the S M T W T F S chips to pick specific days. Examples:

email Sam tomorrow p1
review Q3 deck friday
standup every weekday
pay rent every month p2
1:1 every 2 weeks
call dentist in 3 days
sync with Alex next tue 3pm
You type…It parses…
p1 … p4Priority (P1 highest → red; P4 none)
today, tomorrow, next tue, aug 14, 8/14, in 3 daysDue date
5pm, at 17:00Time of day
every day, every weekday, every friday, weekly, monthly, every 3 days, every 2 weeks, every 3 months, every other weekRecurrence (with intervals)
No date? A task with no due date defaults to today, so it never gets lost. Every task's date chip is clickable — use it (or “📅 Set date”) to open the calendar and reschedule.

Where tasks appear

  • Overdue & Today — anything due today or earlier, in the note view.
  • Upcoming — future-dated tasks, grouped by day.
  • Completed on this note — when you finish a task, it crosses out on the note where you completed it, and never appears on future notes.
  • The Tasks page (top bar) — every open task across all workspaces, grouped by due date, then by priority and workspace. Click a workspace badge to jump there, or ➜ to move a task to another workspace.
  • Completed history — on the Tasks page, switch from Open to Completed for a log of finished tasks grouped by the day you completed them (newest first). Search the text, pick a date range (last 7/30/90 days, this month/year, all time, or a custom span), and filter by workspace and priority. Click a row's filled circle to reopen it (it returns to Open), or ✕ to delete it for good.
  • The 📅 Calendar (top bar) — a month grid of dated tasks, with recurring tasks projected across their future occurrences. Use ‹ › to change month, or Today to jump back.
  • Reminders: give a task a time (e.g. call Sam today 3pm) and, with notifications enabled (🔔), it raises an alert when that time arrives — on desktop and on mobile when the app is installed to your Home Screen (iPhone/iPad need iOS 16.4+). Date-only tasks don't alert.
  • Quick capture anywhere: press ⌘/Ctrl+K and type a task — "Add task: …" appears with the parsed date/priority/repeat.

Completing, repeating, skipping

  • Click the circle to complete a task (click again to reopen).
  • A recurring task rolls forward to its next occurrence when you complete it; the finished occurrence stays crossed-out on that note.
  • ⏭ Skip advances a recurring task to the next occurrence without marking it done.
  • ⚑ cycles priority; the 📅 date chip reschedules; ➜ moves the task to another workspace; ✕ deletes.
  • Picking a destination: ➜ opens a searchable list of your workspaces — type to filter, use ↑/↓ and Enter to choose, or Esc to cancel.
Priorities show as a colored bar on the left of each task: P1 red, P2 amber, P3 blue, P4 none.

7. Writing & the editor

The Ongoing and Meeting Notes areas are rich-text editors. Each is a fixed-height, internally-scrolling box — drag the handle at its bottom-right corner to resize it, and the height is remembered between refreshes (per device).

  • Formatting: bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, bullet & numbered lists, headings, quotes, code, and links from the toolbar.
  • Slash menu: type / at the start of a word (line start or after a space) for headings, lists, tables, quotes, and code blocks — so a stray / mid-word won't trigger it. Dismiss it with Esc or by clicking away.
  • Indent: press Tab to indent — it nests the current bullet/numbered-list item, or indents a paragraph — and Shift+Tab to outdent. (Tab indents inside the editor rather than moving focus; click elsewhere to leave it.)
  • Images: paste, drag-and-drop, or insert with 🖼 in either editor. Drag the corner handle to resize, or use +/-. Images are stored as encrypted attachments (not bloating the note), and images in Ongoing Notes carry forward to the next daily note with their own copy.
  • Tables: insert with ▦.
  • Note links & backlinks: ⧉ links to another note and creates a backlink; each note lists what links to it.
  • Attachments: attach files up to 20 MB below the Meeting Notes; they're stored encrypted like everything else.

Your work saves automatically — watch the Saved ✓ indicator at the bottom of the sidebar. If the same note was changed elsewhere (another tab or a synced device), you'll be offered to keep both copies.

8. Recording & transcription

Record a meeting

In the Meeting Notes section, 🔴 Record captures your mic and the other side (a shared tab or system audio) mixed into a single .wav file, saved as an encrypted attachment. 🖥 Screen records a shared screen/window/tab as a .webm video (with system audio and your mic).

Live transcript

With transcription enabled, a near-live transcript appears in its own area below your notes — timestamped and labelled You vs Them, ordered by when things were actually said.

Transcription is optional and configured under ⚙ Transcription in the Meeting Notes header. For full privacy you can run a local Whisper server (nothing is installed for you — it's opt-in; one click fills the endpoint) so audio never leaves your machine. See docs/local-whisper.md. Recording uses your browser's microphone/screen-share permissions.

AI summary & action items

Once you've configured a summary endpoint, a ✨ Summarize button appears in the Meeting Notes header. It sends this note's notes + transcript to your chosen model and returns a short summary plus a list of action items — each with a ＋ Add task button (any date it mentions, like “friday”, is parsed into the task's due date). You can also insert the summary at the top of your notes.

AI summary is opt-in and off by default — the button stays hidden until you set an endpoint under ⚙️ Settings → AI meeting summary. Point it at a local LLM (e.g. LM Studio / Ollama's OpenAI-compatible /chat/completions) to keep everything on your machine, or a cloud provider (your meeting text is then sent there to summarize). The API key is stored encrypted and only the server ever holds it.

9. Search, tags & favorites

  • Search the top box to find text across every note in every workspace.
  • Operators: narrow a search by combining words with tag:name, in:workspace, is:favorite, is:daily/is:scratch, and has:attachment — e.g. launch in:product has:attachment.
  • Saved searches: run a search, then hit ★ Save this search to pin it (query and all) to a 🔎 Saved searches sidebar section — one click re-runs it anytime.
  • Wiki links: in any editor, type [[ to pick another note and drop an inline link; the linked note shows a Linked from list so connections work both ways.
  • Tags: add #tags to notes; filter results with tag:name. Click a tag on a note to open a cross-workspace view of every note that carries it.
  • Tag bookmarks: in a tag view, hit 🔖 Bookmark tag to pin it to a sidebar section shown in every workspace. Creating a note from a tag view auto-applies the tag and asks which workspace to put it in.
  • Favorites: star a note (the ☆ on the note, or hover it in the sidebar) and find them under ★.
  • Sort the sidebar by Created, Modified, or Name, ascending or descending. Name-sort sinks un-named notes below same-date named ones.

10. Slack & the inbox

Two independent, optional integrations.

Inbox: send yourself tasks (Slack → app)

Message yourself on Slack and have it become a task — even while your computer is asleep. An outside service (Zapier, Make, or a Cloudflare Worker) drops a file into your data folder's inbox/, and the app turns each line into a task (badged 📥) in your chosen workspace the next time it's unlocked.

Pick the target workspace in ⚙ Settings → Inbox. Full setup lives in docs/slack-inbox.md.

Agenda: post your tasks to Slack (app → Slack)

The app can post your due & overdue tasks to a Slack channel via an Incoming Webhook — on demand or auto-daily. Configure it in ⚙ Settings → Slack.

Auto-send is best-effort while the app is open and unlocked. The webhook URL is stored encrypted in your settings.

11. Security & unlocking

  • Encryption at rest: AES-256-GCM envelope encryption; the key is derived from your passphrase with scrypt and only lives in server memory while unlocked.
  • Passphrase & recovery key: change your passphrase or use your recovery key under ⚙ Settings.
  • Biometric unlock: optionally re-unlock with Touch ID / Windows Hello / a device passkey after an auto-lock, instead of retyping your passphrase. It's per-device and opt-in; your passphrase is still required elsewhere. Enable it in ⚙ Settings.
  • Idle auto-lock: the app locks itself after a period of inactivity. Choose the delay — 5 min to 4 hours, or Never — under ⚙ Settings → Auto-lock & sessions (default 15 minutes).
  • Session lifetime: also under ⚙ Settings → Auto-lock & sessions, “Stay signed in for” sets how long your unlocked session stays valid on the server (1 hour to 1 week; default 4 hours). It slides with use; after that long away — tab closed or asleep — you re-enter your passphrase. (The SESSION_TTL env var still sets the default.)
  • Hardening: CSRF tokens, session auth, a strict Content-Security-Policy, anti-clickjacking headers, and login rate-limiting.
Biometric note: passkeys need a real hostname — localhost or a domain over HTTPS, not a raw 127.0.0.1 IP address.

12. Backup, export & sync

Backup, export & restore

Under ⋮ → Backup, export & offline viewer you can download a single encrypted backup of everything and restore it on a fresh install. You can also bulk-export a whole workspace as a ZIP (HTML / Markdown / JSON).

Verify a backup (restore drill): in the same dialog, pick a backup file and hit Verify backup to decrypt-check every entry with your current key — proof it will actually restore, before you need it. Nothing is imported or changed.

Conflict history

If the same note is edited on two devices at once and you choose Keep both during a sync conflict, a copy is made and the event is logged under ⋮ → 🔀 Conflicts, with links to the original and the copy so you can reconcile them later.

Offline viewer — read your notes on your phone, no network

Cove keeps a self-contained meeting-notes-viewer.html in your data folder, refreshed automatically (on startup, shortly after edits, and on shutdown). It's a single read-only HTML file with every note & task that decrypts on-device with your passphrase — no server, no network. So if your data folder is a synced folder (Google Drive / Box / Dropbox / iCloud), the file lands on your phone too: open it there (download it and open in your browser if the Drive/Files preview won't run it), enter your passphrase, and browse and search everything — even when the Cove app itself isn't reachable.

The auto-saved copy omits inline images to stay small. For a copy including images, use ⋮ → Backup, export & offline viewer → Download viewer (with images). Both are point-in-time snapshots; the auto-saved one refreshes itself, and you can set VIEWER_AUTOWRITE=0 to turn the auto-write off.

Export & import a note

Export any note to PDF (print), HTML, Markdown, or JSON. Import a previously exported JSON / HTML / Markdown note back into a workspace.

Export for AI (ChatGPT / Claude)

⋮ → Backup, export & offline viewer → Export for AI packages a whole workspace or everything under a tag into clean Markdown you can upload as knowledge/research to an AI assistant. Choose one combined file (with a table of contents and the workspace's open tasks up top) or a ZIP with one file per note plus an index. Notes are ordered oldest-first so the thread reads chronologically, and rich text (headings, lists, links) is converted to Markdown. Nothing is sent anywhere — the file downloads to your device for you to upload yourself.

Sync across devices

Because the data folder is just files, put it in a cloud-sync folder (Google Drive / Dropbox / Box) and your encrypted vault follows you. Changes to the folder refresh open notes in real time. If two devices edited the same note, saving opens a merge view: each changed section shows an inline word-level diff (removed text struck through, added text highlighted), you pick Keep yours or Keep theirs per section (unchanged sections merge automatically), expand to see the full versions, or keep both as a copy.

Scheduled backups can be configured so you always have a recent snapshot.

13. Install & offline

Cove ships as a PWA — installable from your browser as an app, with an offline app shell so the interface loads even without a connection. Your note data still comes from the local server when it's running.

Offline-safe edits: if a save can't reach the server (network drop, server briefly down), your change isn't lost — the indicator shows “Offline — save pending ⏳” and the edit is stored on your device and retried automatically when you reconnect (and if you try to close the tab while a save is still pending, the browser warns you first). When a newer version of the app takes over while a tab is open, a small “A new version is ready — Reload” prompt appears rather than leaving you on stale code.

Offline reading (opt-in): enable Settings → Offline reading to keep a readable copy of recently-opened notes in this browser. When the server is unreachable, the “server isn’t running” screen shows a 📖 Read cached notes button that opens a read-only view of those copies. Note these copies are stored unencrypted in the browser (unlike your vault), so it's off by default; turning it off clears them.

To install: use your browser's Install app / Add to Home Screen option while viewing the app.

On a phone, the layout adapts: the note list collapses into a drawer you open with the ☰ button (top-left), the search bar drops to its own row, and row actions (star, delete, reschedule) are always tappable rather than hidden behind hover. Tapping a note or switching workspace closes the drawer automatically.

15. Where your data lives

Everything is stored under the app's data directory (set with the DATA_DIR environment variable; defaults to a data/ folder beside the app). Notes and attachments are individual .enc files, encrypted at rest; only a small vault.json key descriptor is plaintext.

<DATA_DIR>/
  vault.json           key descriptor (no secrets in the clear)
  index.json.enc       workspaces, settings, templates
  ws/<id>/             one folder per workspace
    tasks.json.enc     that workspace's tasks
    notes/*.enc        encrypted notes & attachments
  inbox/               drop-zone for Slack/etc. → tasks
To move everything to another machine, copy the whole data directory (and the app) — then unlock with your passphrase.

16. Troubleshooting & FAQ

I forgot my passphrase.

Use your recovery key to unlock, then change your passphrase in Settings. Without either, the data can't be decrypted — by design.

The calendar / date picker doesn't open.

Make sure you're on an up-to-date version. Click the date chip on a task (or “📅 Set date”); the picker opens anchored to the chip. If your browser blocks it, the field still accepts a typed date.

Biometric unlock says “invalid domain”.

Passkeys require localhost, a domain, or HTTPS — not a raw 127.0.0.1 address. Open the app at http://localhost:<port> or a cove.localhost address (the Enable button is hidden on an IP address for this reason).

It adds the passkey, then errors.

Biometric unlock uses the passkey's PRF extension (a.k.a. hmac-secret) to wrap your key. If your device creates the passkey but can't produce a PRF secret, enrollment can't finish. A few things to check:

The failure is usually about which passkey provider handled the passkey — some don't implement PRF:

  • On a Mac with Chrome: Chrome's built-in “this device” passkey provider doesn't support PRF. When the “Create a passkey” prompt appears, click “Save another way” and choose iCloud Keychain (or your iPhone) — those do support PRF. Safari uses iCloud Keychain by default and works too. (Needs macOS 15 / iOS 18 or later for iCloud Keychain PRF.)
  • A password manager — 1Password, Bitwarden, Dashlane — handling passkeys will also fail, since their passkeys don't support PRF. Turn off its passkey / “offer to save” handling for this site, or pick a different option in the prompt.
  • On Windows: pick Windows Hello (recent Windows 11 + current Chrome/Edge). Some older Windows Hello / TPM combinations don't expose PRF.
  • A plug-in security key won't provide PRF. Open over localhost or HTTPS (not a raw IP).
  • After a failed attempt, delete the leftover “Cove” passkey from wherever it was saved (browser/OS/manager passkey settings) before retrying, so the non-PRF one isn't reused.

When it fails, Cove now logs the authenticator's exact response to the browser console (F12 → Console) — that line tells you whether PRF was enabled at all. Either way your passphrase still works, and you can delete the leftover “Cove” passkey in your browser or OS passkey settings.

My Slack message didn't become a task.

The app drains the inbox about once a minute while it's unlocked. Open and unlock the app, and confirm your glue service is writing into the right inbox/ folder. See docs/slack-inbox.md.

The app opens but says “Cove server isn’t running”.

The installed app (PWA) can open from its cached shell, but it still needs the local node server.js running to reach your notes — a browser tab can’t start that itself. Start Cove with ./start.sh (start.bat on Windows, or npm start) and the screen continues on its own. start.sh / start.bat run the server in the background, so you can close the terminal or window afterward; stop it later with ./stop.sh / stop.bat, or reload it with scripts/restart.sh. To avoid the message entirely, set Cove to start at login (see docs/macos-startup.md).

I see an old version after updating.

The app serves the freshest files on reload; a hard refresh clears any cached shell. Installed PWAs update on next launch.

Is anything sent to the cloud?

No — not unless you deliberately sync the data folder or configure the Slack integrations. There are no analytics and no third-party runtime dependencies.
